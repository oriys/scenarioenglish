# Research — Scene 016 Missed boarding / gate closed

> The terminal boarding disruption: the traveller reaches the gate late (or is otherwise not on board when the door closes) and is not allowed on. This scene trains the reality — whether you can still get on, whom to speak to, and how to ask for rebooking/next steps — and makes the important legal distinction between **arriving too late to board** and **denied boarding (bumping)**.

## 1. Research question

What realistically happens when someone misses the gate / boarding closes, can they still join the flight, whom do they talk to, and which rights/outcomes apply — especially the distinction between "arrived late" and "denied boarding"?

The lesson should train the smallest complete task:

> Realise you may have missed boarding → approach the gate agent / customer-service desk → state you missed the flight → ask for the next available flight / rebooking / what happens → act on the answer.

Scene 016 is about **the communication of a missed boarding and getting rebooked.** It is the landing-point of Scenes 012–015 (all the ways you can end up at this desk).

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Two very different situations

It matters which of these happened:

1. **Arrived late / after the boarding gate closed** — the aircraft has (or is closing the) door; the airline may refuse to carry you because you were not at the gate/boarding by the required time. This is **not** "denied boarding" in the compensation sense.
2. **Denied boarding (bumping)** — the airline **overbooked** or swapped to a smaller aircraft and can't seat you even though you checked in and were at the gate on time. This **is** the regulated situation with compensation/assistance rights (UK law / UK261).

Scene 016 is about situation **1** (missing the flight) while making clear that situation 2 has different rights (so learners don't demand compensation for a self-inflicted late arrival).

### 2.2 What happens at the gate / desk

- Walking up after the flight has left (or the gate closed) → you cannot board; the agent will explain the aircraft is gone / the door is closed.
- You are directed to **customer service / the airline's transfer/help desk** (or the same agent rebooks).
- The airline will typically offer **rebooking to the next available flight to your destination** — this may be free of charge for a same-booking connection problem, or subject to a fee/fare difference when the missed flight was your own fault (change fees), depending on the fare type.
- If it was your **own late arrival**, you don't get denied-boarding compensation; you pay the airline's change/rebooking terms for that fare.
- Even in genuine denied-boarding (bump) cases, the CAA confirms you're still entitled to **choose an alternative flight or a refund** plus care/assistance while waiting.

### 2.3 The essential learner output — signalling urgency

When late, the slightly-panicked passenger's best move is a short, clear statement so staff prioritise and understand:

- `I think I've missed my flight.`
- `The gate was closed before I got here.`
- `Can I still get on?`
- `What are my options? / What happens now?`
- `Can you put me on the next flight?`

### 2.4 End of the task

Success = a rebooking to a later flight (or a clear explanation of the alternative/refund), and the traveller knows whom to ask and what to say about money/compensation without overclaiming.

## 3. Real-world English

### 3.1 Staff wording

- `I'm sorry, the gate is closed / the flight has departed.`
- `You've missed your flight to ___.`
- `We can't let you board now — the aircraft has left.`
- `Please go to our customer service desk.`
- `We can look for the next available flight.`
- `There's a change fee for this ticket.` / `There'll be a fare difference.`
- `Would you like to be rebooked or get a refund?`

### 3.2 Learner output

- `I think I missed my flight.`
- `I arrived at the gate too late — is the flight gone?`
- `Can you put me on the next flight to ___?`
- `What are my options?`
- `Is there anything you can do?`
- `Will there be a fee for that?`
- `What time does the next flight leave?`
- `Do you cover a hotel if I have to wait?` (care/assistance in bump cases)

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Confusing "arrived late" with "denied boarding"

Real and consequential: a late arrival is not the regulated bump; demanding denied-boarding compensation when you were late is wrong and will be refused. Train the distinction and the honest framing.

### 4.2 Not saying you're about to miss it early enough (missed chance to be prioritised)

If a connection/lateness is foreseeable, flag it (P064 `I think I'm going to miss + noun`). Worth training even though Scene 016 is the post-hoc version.

### 4.3 Not going to the right desk / not asking for rebooking

Passengers may stand at a closed gate waiting. Train: go to customer service/rebook, ask `Can you put me on the next flight?`.

### 4.4 Not asking about fees / not knowing the fare type

Change fees and fare differences depend on ticket/fare. Train `Will there be a fee?` rather than assuming free rebooking.

### 4.5 Mishearing the next-flight details

Train confirmation: `Did you say 8 o'clock / flight BA456?`, `Could you write it down?`.

## 5. Current rules / policy-sensitive facts

`policy_sensitive: true`

`last_verified: 2026-08`

- **If you miss the flight because you arrived at the gate after it closed / were not on board in time, the airline can refuse to carry you as a late arrival — this is NOT "denied boarding" for compensation.** Denied boarding (bumping) applies when you checked in on time and had a seat but the airline couldn't carry you (overbooking/smaller aircraft).
- In a **bump** case under UK law (UK261 predecessor), you are still entitled to **choose re-routing (alternative flight) or a refund**, and **care/assistance** while waiting (refreshments, communication, accommodation if overnight). Compensation amounts for involuntary bumps are distance-based (£110–£520 bands).
- **Reasonable grounds to refuse carriage** include health, safety, security or **inadequate documentation** — a separate set of reasons from bumping.
- Rebooking after your own late arrival typically follows the airline's **change-fee / fare-difference** rules for your fare type; it is not automatically free.
- Whether you can still make the flight depends on the airline/airport and how close the aircraft is to leaving; once the door is closed the plane won't wait (BA: "we can't delay the aircraft's departure to wait for late passengers", but they may try to contact you if they have your details).

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 016

- recognising the gate-is-closed / flight-has-gone reality;
- the urgent signalling opener (`I think I've missed my flight`);
- who to ask and where to go (agent → customer-service desk);
- asking for rebooking / the next flight / options;
- asking about fees and care (hotel if overnight, in bump cases);
- the honest distinction: late arrival ≠ denied-boarding compensation;
- confirming next-flight times/numbers (repair).

### Move out of Scene 016

- finding the gate / walk time → Scene 012;
- boarding groups/scanning → Scene 013;
- gate-change response → Scene 014;
- gate-check of cabin bags → Scene 015;
- full compensation claims/delay-cancellation depth → Scenes 022–023.

### Reusable patterns (PATTERN_BANK.md)

- P064 — `I think I'm going to miss + noun` (early warning)
- P065 — `Is there anything you can do?`
- P066 — `Could you put me on + noun?`
- P067 — `Will I be + past participle?` (charged? covered?)
- P063 — `What happens if + 情况?`
- P062 — `Who should I speak to about + noun?`
- Repair phrases.

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

To keep Scene 016 honest it will NOT train:

- a claim that rebooking is always free (fees depend on fare type);
- full compensation tables (bump amounts belong in disruption scenes, and are only referenced to make the late-vs-bump distinction);
- the idea that arriving late entitles you to denied-boarding compensation (it does not);
- seat-finding on board (017) or baggage claims.

The learner should instead leave Scene 016 able to:

1. recognise they've missed boarding and act fast;
2. say it simply (`I think I've missed my flight — can you help?`);
3. get to the right desk and ask to be rebooked / for the next step;
4. ask about fees, timing and (in a bump) care/refund options without overclaiming;
5. confirm the next flight's details clearly.
