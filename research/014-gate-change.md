# Research — Scene 014 Gate change

> A common, high-stakes disruption in the departure lounge: the boarding gate **changes** after it was first shown. The learner must notice the update (screen and/or announcement), understand the change, and act quickly. This scene is deliberately scoped: it trains **noticing and responding to a gate change**, and connects to finding the gate (012) and missed-boarding (016) if the traveller does not respond in time.

## 1. Research question

What does a traveller need to see/hear, understand and do when their boarding gate changes, and how is that different from the general "find the gate" scene (012)?

The lesson should train the smallest complete task:

> Be in the departure lounge → notice the gate has changed (screen flag or announcement) → confirm the new gate → go to the new gate in time → board.

Scene 014 is about **monitoring for and reacting to a change.** It does NOT re-teach general gate-finding (012) or the panic recovery of a missed gate (016).

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Gate changes are routine and expected

Gates routinely change at airports because of operational reasons (aircraft, stand availability, weather, knock-on delays). Heathrow's departure-lounge guidance already tells passengers to check the departure screens **"regularly"** because flight information can change; the gate number shown around **45 minutes before departure** is not necessarily final.

### 2.2 How the change is communicated

Two channels, often used together:

- **Screens:** the departure board updates the gate column (the flight's row now shows a different gate, and possibly a status like `Gate changed`). This is why Heathrow/BA tell passengers to check screens **"at regular intervals"** — the app/boarding-pass gate can be stale, so the live screen is authoritative.
- **Announcement / paging:** a tannoy call such as `Passengers for flight ___, please proceed to gate ___` or `Flight ___ has changed to gate ___`. The earlier note recorded the real, widely-heard format: **"Passengers for flight two-four-eight to Manchester, please proceed to gate nine for boarding."**

### 2.3 What the traveller must do

- confirm the **flight number** on the screen before relying on a gate;
- capture the **new gate** and the **remaining time**;
- head there promptly (allow walk/train time);
- if unsure, confirm with a gate/info agent (`Is this the right gate for flight ___?`).

### 2.4 End of the task

Success = reach the correct new gate and board. If the change is spotted too late or the walk is too long, the traveller may miss boarding (→ Scene 016).

## 3. Real-world English

### 3.1 Signs / screens / announcements

- Gate changed / Check screens / New gate
- `Passengers for flight ___ , please proceed to gate ___`
- `Flight ___ has been moved to gate ___`
- `Please re-check the departure boards`
- Now boarding / Final call (at the new gate)

### 3.2 High-probability announcement phrasing (intent families)

1. announce a move of flight to a new gate;
2. ask passengers to proceed/board at the new gate;
3. caveat to keep checking screens.

Representative natural wording:

- `Passengers for flight BA123, please proceed to Gate 22.`
- `Flight BA123 has moved to Gate 22.`
- `Due to an airfield change, flight ___ will now board from gate ___.`
- `Please check the boards for any changes to your gate.`
- `This is the final call for flight ___ at Gate 22.`

Learner questions/statements:

- `Has my gate changed?`
- `Is this the right gate for flight ___?`
- `Where has the flight moved to?`
- `I heard an announcement for flight ___ — is that us?`
- `How do I get to the new gate from here?`

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Relying on the printed/app gate and not re-checking the screen

The single most important risk: the boarding-pass/app gate goes stale. Train the habit "check the screens regularly."

### 4.2 Mishearing the flight number in an announcement

High listen-pressure: flight numbers can sound alike. Train `Did you say flight ___?` / confirming the flight number.

### 4.3 Acting on the wrong flight's announcement

Announcements for other flights are common. Train matching to **your** flight number + destination before moving.

### 4.4 Underestimating the new gate's distance

Same as Scene 012 but under time pressure. Train `How long is it to gate ___?` and moving quickly.

### 4.5 Not confirming when unsure

Train asking a gate/info agent rather than guessing (`Is this the right gate for ___?`, P057).

## 5. Current rules / policy-sensitive facts

`policy_sensitive: true`

`last_verified: 2026-08`

- **Gate numbers can and do change**; the number shown ~45 min before departure (Heathrow) or shortly before the flight opens (BA LHR T5) is not guaranteed final.
- Airports/airlines advise **re-checking the departure screens regularly**; apps/boarding passes can be outdated, so the live screen/announcement is authoritative.
- Announcement phrasing varies by airport/airline, but the pattern **"Passengers for flight ___, please proceed to gate ___"** is near-universal to hear (and to train for listening).
- No universal "time left after change to walk" exists; distance depends on airport/stand (satellite gates can be /~20 min at LHR).

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 014

- the habit of re-checking screens/apps because gates change;
- recognising a `Gate changed` screen state;
- understanding the announcement format (`Passengers for flight ___, please proceed to gate ___`);
- confirming **your** flight number in an announcement;
- asking for/confirming the new gate and the route/time (`Which gate? How long is it?`);
- moving promptly without panicking.

### Move out of Scene 014

- general gate-finding/walk-time language → Scene 012;
- boarding groups/scanning → Scene 013;
- cabin-bag gate-check → Scene 015;
- missed-the-gate recovery / rebooking → Scene 016.

### Reusable patterns (PATTERN_BANK.md)

- P055 — `Which gate / desk / lane do I need?`
- P056 — `Has + noun + been called yet?`
- P057 — `Is this the right + noun + for me?`
- P016 — `Did you say + information?` (confirm a flight number)
- P063 — `What happens if + 情况?`
- Universal repair phrases.

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

To keep Scene 014 tight it will NOT train:

- a full gate-finding/walk-time scene (012);
- boarding groups (013);
- whether a changed gate entitles compensation (variance/rights nuances belong to disruption scenes 022-023);
- rebooking/refund recovery (016).

The learner should instead leave Scene 014 able to:

1. know gates change and habitually re-check screens/apps;
2. spot a `Gate changed` screen state;
3. understand the tannoy pattern `Passengers for flight ___, please proceed to gate ___`;
4. confirm it's **their** flight (number + destination) before moving;
5. find and reach the new gate in time, asking if unsure.
