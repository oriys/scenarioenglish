# Research — Scene 010 E-gates and manual passport control

> This note covers the **first of the two "departure control" scenes (01.03)**: arriving in the UK and clearing passport control via the automatic **eGates (ePassport gates)** or a **manual Border Force officer** at the desk. It handles the task, the eligibility, the two failure/referral paths, and the learner's communication at the desk when an eGate sends them to an officer. Scene 011 owns the actual Q&A at the desk.

## 1. Research question

What does an arriving traveller need to understand and do at UK passport control, and how should Scene 010 split "use the electronic gates or go to a manual lane" from the later "answer the officer's questions" scene (011)?

The lesson should train the smallest complete task:

> Arrive in the arrivals hall → follow the passport-control lanes/egates → use an eGate if eligible and it works → (if not) go to a Border Force officer → hand over your passport → continue past passport control to Arrivals/Baggage Reclaim.

Scene 010 is about **choosing and using the right entry channel and coping with an eGate that fails.** It does NOT teach the full interview language (011) or prohibited-goods/customs (beyond a handoff).

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Two channels: eGates vs a manual officer

At UK airports passengers reach a passport-control hall with (typically) signs splitting travellers between:

- the **eGates (ePassport gates)** — self-service gates that compare your face to the chip in your passport; and
- **manual Border Force desks** — an officer checks your identity document and, where applicable, asks why you are visiting.

GOV.UK and the airports describe eGates as "usually the quickest way to enter the UK" (Heathrow). There are **over 290 eGates at 15 UK air and rail ports** (GOV.UK "faster travel").

Real-world learner tasks:

- get passport/travel documents ready (removed from a cover, open at the photo page);
- decide whether they are eligible to use an eGate;
- use the gate correctly (scan the page, look at the screen, wait for the green light);
- recognise that the gate "didn't work" and be ready to be directed to an officer;
- know which lane if they are not eligible (manual desk / "All other passports").

### 2.2 eGate eligibility (current, and changeable)

GOV.UK (Entering the UK — at border control) and the "Guide to faster travel" give the current eligibility:

For **British citizens** — eGates if the passport has a biometric symbol on the cover and the passenger is **8 years old or over**. 8–17 year-olds must be accompanied by an adult.

For **EU / Switzerland / Norway / Iceland / Liechtenstein** — eGates if the passport has a biometric symbol, the passenger is **8 or over**, and they have a **valid digital immigration status or record of a digital exemption**.

For **nationals of other countries** — eGates if the passport has a biometric symbol, the passenger is **8 or over**, has a **valid digital immigration status/exemption**, and holds a passport from an **eligible country**. Current eligible countries list: **Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, Singapore, South Korea, United States**.

Members of the **Registered Traveller Service** may also be able to use the eGates.

Common to all: the passenger must be **120cm (3ft 11in) or taller**, and eGates use **facial recognition** to check identity against the passport photo.

The **age/eligibility rules have changed recently and continue to change**: from **8 July 2026**, children aged **8 and 9** became eligible to use UK eGates (previously the minimum was 10). GOV.UK and airports (e.g. Heathrow) now publish a three-tier table: Under 8 = no; 8–9 = yes with an adult, ≥120cm, eligible biometric passport; 10+ = yes if eligible.

Course rule: teach the **eligibility categories and the vocabulary**, but explicitly mark the **age / country list / ETA as changeable**, and never present one list as permanent.

### 2.3 Using the gate and the family order

Family/group guidance (GOV.UK "faster travel"):

- travelling as a family, move through passport control **together**;
- adults travelling alone with children under 18 should use the eGates **first**;
- if 2+ adults travel with children: **Adult 1 → child/children → final adult**;
- remove hats, headphones, sunglasses; put phones away; remove the passport from its cover, open at the photo page; **look directly into the screen until it turns green**.

### 2.4 The manual desk as the fallback

Anyone not eligible, taller/under-age, whose gate fails, or who wants a stamp, sees a Border Force officer. GOV.UK is explicit that some travellers **must** see an officer and cannot use eGates (e.g. a Temporary Work – Creative Worker arriving on a certificate of sponsorship who needs a passport stamp).

### 2.5 End of the task

Success = past passport control, then follow signs to **Arrivals / Baggage Reclaim** (onward travel; customs is a separate step).

## 3. Real-world English

### 3.1 Signs / labels

- Passport control / Immigration
- UK, EU and EEA / UK & EU / British and EEA (lane names vary by airport)
- All other passports / Other nationalities
- eGates / ePassport gates / Electronic passport control
- "Use the eGates" / "Have your passport ready"
- Registered Traveller

### 3.2 Documents / objects

- passport / travel document / national ID
- biometric passport / chip / ePassport
- electronic gate / eGate
- facial recognition
- photo page
- boarding pass
- ETA (electronic travel authorisation) / eVisa / digital status

### 3.3 High-probability staff intentions

The gate itself gives machine prompts; an officer at the manual desk gives short instructions. Representative natural wording:

- `Please have your passport ready — photo page up.`
- `Use the eGate, please. / This gate, please.`
- `Place your passport on the reader / scan your passport.`
- `Look at the screen, please.`
- `That gate didn't work — go to see an officer. / Please go to the desk.`
- `Your eGate isn't reading — come this way.`
- `May I see your passport, please?` (manual desk)
- `You're all set — welcome to the UK.`
- `How long are you staying? / What's the purpose of your visit?` (→ Scene 011)

Learner output prompts:

- `Do I go through the eGates?`
- `Can I use the eGates with this passport?`
- `This is my passport.`
- `I'm sorry — the gate didn't let me through.` (handoff)
- `Which lane should I use?`
- `Where is passport control?`

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Not eligible / underage / too short

Real and common: an eGate refuses travel rules. GOV.UK's manual is the reliable guide. Course rule: teach recognition of "check whether you're eligible" rather than memorising the list.

### 4.2 The gate rejects the passport / the user (referral to an officer)

GOV.UK ("Reasons you may not get through eGates") lists the real reasons, which double as the failure taxonomy:

- **border security** — further checks on the document/nationality/identity or eligibility;
- **digital permission** — needs an ETA/eVisa/digital exemption, or the digital status is no longer valid;
- **eGate error** — the machine itself faulted;
- **identification error** — appearance changed vs the photo, or the passenger didn't look at the screen until it turned green, or kept on a hat/headphones/face mask/glasses;
- **passport or microchip error** — damaged or worn passport/microchip;
- **safeguarding** — need to establish the adult–child relationship;
- **user error** — not a biometric passport, cover still on, wrong page, not pressed firmly.

Course rule: this is **the** high-frequency failure worth training. The learner should know the gate may say nothing useful and an officer appears or directs them to a desk — that is normal, not an accusation.

### 4.3 Not having documents ready / putting a phone or headphones on at the gate

GOV.UK advises removing covers/hats/headphones/sunglasses and putting phones away. Worth a reminder, low language load.

### 4.4 Frozen at the desk / not knowing the officer's question triggers

This belongs mostly to Scene 011, but Scene 010 should plant the handoff: "they will ask why you're here" so the learner is not surprised.

## 5. Current rules / policy-sensitive facts

`policy_sensitive: true`

`last_verified: 2026-08`

- eGates are self-service gates that use facial recognition; they are faster, not universal.
- Eligibility currently requires a **biometric passport**, **age 8+** (8–17 accompanied by an adult), **height ≥120cm**, and (for many non-UK travellers) a **valid digital immigration status / ETA / eVisa / exemption**. Non-EU eligible country list currently: Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, Singapore, South Korea, USA.
- **Dynamic:** from **8 July 2026** children aged **8–9** became eligible (minimum was previously 10); **ETA** now applies to many visitors and currently costs **£20**. The eligible-country list, minimum age and ETA scope have changed over time and will keep changing — do NOT present as fixed.
- If the gate rejects you, you are referred to a **Border Force officer**; common reasons are machine/identification/passport/digital-status/security issues (see GOV.UK "Reasons you may not get through eGates").
- Some entrants **must** see an officer (e.g. needing a passport stamp for the Creative Worker concession).
- Lanes/labels vary by airport ("UK, EU, EEA" vs "All other passports"). Source-audience note: GOV.UK and Heathrow have slightly different eligible-country phrasing; use GOV.UK as authoritative and flag airport variation.

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 010

- recognising the passport-control area and the two channels;
- preparing documents (photo page, no cover, hat/headphones/sunglasses off);
- reading/using the eGate (scan, look at screen, wait for green);
- deciding eligibility (with explicit "rules can change — check");
- handling an eGate that fails and being sent to an officer (the 7 reason families);
- knowing some travellers must use the desk (stamp cases);
- one-liner handoff to the officer(s) and "they will ask why you're here" → Scene 011.

### Move out of Scene 010

- answering the officer's questions in depth → Scene 011;
- customs / prohibited goods → later / 009's list doesn't apply here;
- specific ETA application steps → not taught (just "you may need an ETA").

### Reusable patterns (PATTERN_BANK.md)

- P055 — `Which gate / desk / lane do I need?`
- P057 — `Is this the right + noun + for me?`
- P063 — `What happens if + 情况?` (e.g. the gate fails)
- Repair patterns (universal section).

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

To keep Scene 010 accurate and reusable it will NOT require memorising:

- the exact eGate eligible-country list or the exact minimum age as a permanent fact (both change);
- the full Border Force interview (Scene 011);
- ETA application mechanics or fees as fixed rules;
- all lane sign wording (varies by airport).

The learner should instead leave Scene 010 able to:

1. find passport control and choose eGates vs a manual desk;
2. prepare their passport and use an eGate correctly;
3. stay calm and know what to do when an eGate rejects them;
4. hand over to a Border Force officer with a clear, short opening;
5. know that eligibility rules change and to "check before you travel".
