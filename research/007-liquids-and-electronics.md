# Research — Scene 007 Liquids, Electronics & Power Banks

> This note covers the second security scene: what goes through the scanner in your bag — liquids, electronic devices (laptop/tablet), and power banks / spare lithium batteries. The liquid rule is **currently changing across UK airports**, so this note is especially policy-sensitive and must be treated as a "check the current airport rule" lesson, not a fixed national standard.

## 1. Research question

What does a traveller need to understand about liquids, electronic devices and power banks at UK security, and how should the course handle a rule that currently differs airport-by-airport and is in active transition (2024–2026+)?

The lesson should train the smallest usable task:

> Go to the screening point prepared → place liquids/electronics in the bag (or take them out, depending on the airport) → put the bag on the belt → pass through → collect it. If an item is wrong, respond calmly and co-operate.

The core skill is **"know the rules apply differently; ask and confirm at your departure airport"** rather than memorising a single number.

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Liquids — the changing UK position (critical)

For years the universal UK rule was: liquids in hand luggage in containers of **no more than 100ml**, placed in a single transparent resealable plastic bag (≈20cm × 20cm, max 1 litre) shown to staff.

Timeline that matters for teaching:

- **2022** — UK government announced the 100ml rule would be scrapped as new CT scanners rolled out (target June 2024).
- **By June 2024** — a handful of smaller UK airports (London City, Southend, Teesside, Leeds Bradford) installed CT scanners and raised the limit to 2 litres.
- **June 2024** — the UK government **temporarily reinstated the 100ml cap** at those airports (a security rethink; the EU also reverted in July 2024). Through most of 2024–2025 the practical UK default was again 100ml.
- **Late 2025 → early 2026 (phased, airport-by-airport)** — the Department for Transport / CAA began allowing airports whose scanners are fully rolled out to **raise the limit back up to 2 litres per container**. By **January 2026** Heathrow completed its rollout and now allows containers **up to 2 litres** with liquids left **in** the cabin bag, laptops/electronics left in the bag, and **no clear plastic bag** required.
- As of **last_verified: 2026-08**, airports confirmed at the 2-litre level include **Heathrow, Gatwick, Edinburgh, Birmingham, Bristol, Belfast** (and more may follow). Most other UK airports still work to the **100ml container cap** (DfT's public guidance is to "assume 100ml unless the airport advises otherwise").

Teaching point 1 (policy): The limit is airport-specific and changing. **Do not teach one number as UK-wide.** Teach the three things every traveller should do — check your departure airport's rules, follow the officer's instruction, and re-check the rules before the return flight (returning from abroad may differ).

Teaching point 2 (language): Regardless of policy, the functional English is the same — confirm the cap (`How much liquid can I take?`), understand `in a clear bag` or `leave it in your bag`, and co-operate if something is over.

### 2.2 Electronics (laptops / tablets / devices)

- At airports with 100ml rules in place, laptops/tablets typically had to be **removed from the bag** to a separate tray.
- At airports where the new scanners are fully live (e.g. Heathrow, Gatwick), laptops/tablets/other large electronics **can stay in the bag**.
- All electronic devices must be **fully charged**: if a device cannot be switched on when asked, it may not be allowed on board.
- Reusable metal / insulated bottles must be **emptied** before security (refill after).

Teaching point: Laptop in-or-out is another airport- and phase-dependent detail; train the question and the "follow the officer" rule, not a permanent answer.

### 2.3 Power banks & spare lithium batteries (follows Scene 001)

UK CAA rules (and airline policies such as British Airways) for power banks / spare lithium batteries:

- **Carry-on / hand baggage only.** Power banks and spare lithium batteries are forbidden in checked baggage.
- Spare batteries must be **individually protected** against short circuit (original packaging, taped terminals, or a separate pouch).
- **No more than two power banks per person** (room is open to airline/operator approval for larger-capacity units).
- Capacity: up to **100 Wh** (lithium-ion) or 2 g lithium content needs no approval; **100–160 Wh** requires airline approval.
- Do not recharge power banks / use them to charge devices on board (varies by airline).
- Power banks (up to 2) are generally allowed; e-cigarettes/vapes are hand-luggage-only, carried on the person, not in hold.

Teaching point: The learner must understand `power bank`, `spare battery`, `check-in`, `cabin/hand baggage`, `take it out`, `carry it on board`. Do NOT require memorising watt-hour thresholds — just the direction (in the cabin, protected) and the habit of checking.

## 3. Real-world English

### 3.1 Recognition vocabulary

- liquids / aerosols / gels
- container / bottle
- 100ml / 2000ml (2 litres)
- transparent / clear resealable plastic bag
- laptop / tablet / large electronic devices
- power bank / spare battery
- cabin / hand baggage vs hold / checked baggage
- security screening point / belt / tray

### 3.2 High-probability staff intentions

1. tell you to empty liquids into a separate bag (still at 100ml airports);
2. tell you to leave liquids in your bag (new-scanner airports);
3. ask you to remove the laptop/tablet (some airports);
4. ask you to switch a device on (to prove it works);
5. ask you to take the power bank/battery out or confirm it's in your hand baggage;
6. direct you to step through and collect belongings.

Representative natural wording:

- `Please take your liquids out and put them in the bag.`
- `Leave your liquids in your bag today.`
- `Could you remove your laptop from the bag, please?`
- `Please switch the tablet on for me.`
- `You can only bring one clear plastic bag.`
- `That container is more than 100ml — it can't go through here.`
- `Is your device charged? I need to switch it on.`

Learner output prompts:

- `Is there a limit on how much liquid I can take?` (P051)
- `Do I need to take my laptop out?` (P053)
- `Can I bring my power bank in my hand luggage?` (P004/P050)
- `Can I leave my liquids in the bag here?`

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Over-100ml container at a 100ml airport

Very common and a real cause of confiscation. Train: understand the problem, be polite, check whether it can be returned/checked/surrendered (handled more in Scene 009, but the recognition of "over the limit" belongs here).

### 4.2 Not charging a device

A device that won't switch on can be refused on board. Worth one reminder in UK English, not a lecture.

### 4.3 Assuming the rule is the same at two airports

The Heathrow page explicitly warns: "Many airports outside Heathrow still have a 100ml liquids limit. Check the rules before your return journey." This is a genuine, high-frequency point and worth teaching as "always re-check."

### 4.4 Power bank in checked baggage / unprotected

For some travellers this is the first time it comes up. Scene 007 should make the "hand baggage, protected" direction clear without turning into a dangerous-goods lecture.

## 5. Current rules / policy-sensitive facts

`policy_sensitive: true`

`last_verified: 2026-08`

- **Liquids are airport-specific and in transition.**
  - Confirmed 2-litre airports as of early 2026: Heathrow (T2–5), Gatwick, Edinburgh, Birmingham, Bristol, Belfast — liquids and laptops can usually stay in the bag, no clear plastic bag.
  - Most other UK airports still cap containers at 100ml and may still require a clear bag.
  - gov.uk's guidance: check your departure airport and any transfer/return airports; **assume 100ml unless advised otherwise**.
- The 100ml rule was **temporarily reinstated in June 2024** and has only been lifted airport-by-airport; full UK harmonisation is ongoing and has repeatedly slipped.
- Laptops/electronics in-bag vs out follows the same scanner rollout; ask/confirm.
- All electronic devices must be charged; an unswitchable device may be refused.
- Power banks: **hand/cabin baggage only**, individually protected, **max two per person**, ≤100 Wh without airline approval (100–160 Wh needs approval). Spare lithium batteries same direction. Forbidden in checked baggage.
- E-cigarettes/vapes: hand-luggage/person only, not in hold.
- Empty reusable metal/insulated bottles before security.

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 007

- the idea that the liquid/electronics rule differs by airport and is changing — "ask and confirm";
- liquids vocabulary and the confirming questions (`Is there a limit…`, `Can I leave them in my bag?`, `Do I need a clear bag?`);
- electronic devices: laptop in-or-out as a "follow the officer / confirm" point; devices fully charged;
- power bank / spare battery: correct that it's hand baggage, protected, max two — reuse Scene 001 language;
- polite handling when an item is over the limit (pointing to Scene 009 for what to do with it);
- the return-flight reminder (re-check before coming home).

### Move out of Scene 007

- sign/queue navigation → Scene 006;
- the secondary/manual bag-search procedure → Scene 008;
- prohibited items list and confiscation disposition → Scene 009;
- watt-hour calculations → NOT taught (identification only).

### Reusable patterns (PATTERN_BANK.md)

- P051 — `Is there a limit on + noun?` (J.)
- P053 — `Do I need to take + noun + out?` (J.)
- P050 — `Am I allowed to + action?` (J.)
- P004 — `Can I + action?`
- P003 — `Could you + action, please?`

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

To keep Scene 007 useful and honest about changing rules, it will NOT require memorising:

- a single UK-wide liquid limit (there is none currently);
- watt-hour / g-lithium thresholds for power banks (identification only);
- a permanent answer to "does my laptop come out?" (airport-dependent);
- a permanent answer to "do I need a clear plastic bag?" (airport-dependent);
- which exact airports are live at 2 litres (this changes).

The learner should instead leave Scene 007 able to:

1. ask and confirm the current liquid/electronics rule at any airport;
2. understand `in a clear bag` vs `leave it in your bag`;
3. handle the message "that's over the limit" politely;
4. correctly state that power banks/spare batteries go in hand baggage, protected;
5. know to re-check the rules before their return journey.
