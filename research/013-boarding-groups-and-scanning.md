# Research — Scene 013 Boarding groups, queuing and scanning

> This scene is the operational heart of boarding: how passengers are ordered into the aircraft. The learner must understand the **boarding-pass group number**, what group they are in, when to join the queue, and the moment their **boarding pass is scanned** at the gate. It sits between finding the gate (012) and the two disruptive boarding events (014 gate change, 016 missed the gate) plus the bag gate-check (015).

## 1. Research question

How do airlines order boarding, what does the learner see on the boarding pass and hear at the gate, and which parts belong here (group order + scanning) vs in Scenes 014–016?

The lesson should train the smallest complete task:

> Arrive at the gate with time to spare → note your boarding group on the boarding pass → wait for your group to be called → join the queue when your group is called → present/hold out your boarding pass → have it scanned → board the aircraft.

Scene 013 is about **reading your group, queueing in order, and the scan.** It does NOT teach gate-change response (014), bag gate-checks (015), or missed-boarding recovery (016).

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Group boarding is standard

Airlines board by **group/zone**. British Airways describes a **group boarding system "on all our flights"** and prints a **group number on the boarding pass** ("showing your boarding priority"). The gate agent calls groups in order; you should "be at the gate on time, ready to board the aircraft when your group is called."

Course rule: the **concept** (a number/order on the pass, called by gate staff, join the queue) is universal and stable; the **specific number of groups, names and ordering** vary by airline and even by route/year — teach the concept, not one airline's full table as permanent.

### 2.2 Reading the groups (worked example, not a rule)

BA's own current grouping (short haul / long haul examples):

- **Priority groups 1–3**: First / Club (business), premium economy, elites/status members next.
- **Economy groups 4–5 (short haul) / 4–6 (long haul)**: boarded **by seat row, rear to front**.

Status and cabin members can still use a **priority lane** if their group has been called. Group numbers can differ within a travelling party; BA says travel companions can board together at the gate. **Pre-boarding** is offered for customers with additional needs, and families with infants/small children are invited to board first.

### 2.3 The queue and the scan

At the gate, passengers queue; the gate agent (or an automated reader) **scans the boarding-pass barcode/QR** (paper or phone), confirms it, and the passenger proceeds toward the aircraft. Boarding passes must be **ready** at the scan point — expecting "Have your boarding pass ready." Mobile passes are widely accepted (airline help pages: hold the pass QR under the reader).

### 2.4 End of the task

Success = the pass scans, the traveller steps onto the aircraft (seat-finding and stowing are Scene 017).

## 3. Real-world English

### 3.1 Documents / signs / announcements

- boarding pass / boarding card / group number / zone
- priority / pre-boarding
- Queue here / Wait for your group to be called
- Have your boarding pass ready
- Now boarding (group) / We are now inviting groups 4 to 5 to board
- scan / reader / barcode / QR code

### 3.2 High-probability staff intentions

1. call groups in order;
2. direct passengers to queue by group;
3. ask to have the boarding pass ready;
4. scan and confirm;
5. offer pre-boarding / priority lane.

Representative natural wording:

- `Now boarding groups 4 and 5.`
- `Group 3, you can now board.`
- `Please have your boarding pass ready.`
- `Scan here, please.`
- `That's fine — go through, please.`
- `You're in group 7 — please wait until your group is called.`
- `Pre-boarding for passengers needing extra time.`

Learner questions/statements:

- `What group am I in?`
- `Can I board now? / Should I wait for my group?`
- `Where do I go?`
- `Do I scan here?`
- `I'm travelling with my family — can we board together?`

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Not knowing which group you're in

Real and common — passengers queue early or at the wrong time. Train reading the group number and asking `What group am I in?`.

### 4.2 Queue-jumping / not recognising your group was called

Real (complainants describe people boarding when "groups 1–3" were announced). Train the listening for group numbers and the courteous way to confirm.

### 4.3 Not being ready at the scanner (fumbling the phone/paper)

Real and blocks the queue. Train `Have your boarding pass ready` and the mobile-pass scan habit.

### 4.4 Confusing group with seat row / not boarding together

Train that group ≠ seat, and that companions can often board together even with different groups (BA explicitly).

### 4.5 Assuming a fixed group scheme

Wrong group layouts exist across airlines; avoid teaching a single permanent table.

## 5. Current rules / policy-sensitive facts

`policy_sensitive: true`

`last_verified: 2026-08`

- BA boards by **group number printed on the boarding pass** on all flights; economy boards **rear-to-front by seat row**; priority groups 1–3 precede economy (BA examples, current as verified). **The number/order of groups changes** (BA has simplified its economy groups) and differs by airline — treat the example scheme as illustrative.
- **Pre-boarding** is offered for customers with additional needs; families with infants/small children (under 2 / in pushchairs) are invited to board first and are asked to be at the gate earlier (BA example ~50 minutes before departure) — airline-specific.
- The **boarding-pass scan** (paper or phone QR/barcode) is the final check at the gate; mobile boarding passes are widely accepted. Specific reader wording varies by airport/airline.

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 013

- reading the group number on the boarding pass;
- recognising your group being called (numbers under listening pressure);
- queueing in order / waiting for your group;
- having the boarding pass ready and the phone/QR scan;
- the courtesy of boarding with companions who are in a different group;
- asking `What group am I in?` / `Can I board now?`.

### Move out of Scene 013

- finding the gate and walk time → Scene 012 (already taught);
- responding to a gate change → Scene 014;
- cabin-bag gate-check → Scene 015;
- missed-the-gate recovery → Scene 016;
- seat-finding/stowing on board → Scene 017.

### Reusable patterns (PATTERN_BANK.md)

- P003 — `Could you + action, please?`
- P055 — `Which gate / desk / lane do I need?`
- P056 — `Has + noun + been called yet?`
- P057 — `Is this the right + noun + for me?`
- Repair patterns (universal).

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

To keep Scene 013 accurate it will NOT train:

- one airline's full boarding-group table as a permanent rule (it changes and varies);
- the entire status/elite point structure;
- how to respond to a gate change (014) or missed-boarding (016);
- seat-finding and stowing luggage (017).

The learner should instead leave Scene 013 able to:

1. read their group number and know when to queue;
2. listen for their group being called (numbers under pressure);
3. have the boarding pass ready and scan it at the gate;
4. board with companions who hold different group numbers;
5. calmly ask `What group am I in?` / `Can I board now?` if unsure.
