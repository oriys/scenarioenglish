# Research — Scene 012 Finding the gate and confirming your boarding time

> This note opens the **boarding sub-theme (01.04, Scenes 012–016)**: after security, the traveller is in the **departure lounge** and must find their **gate** and leave enough time. Scene 012 is about reading the departure screens, locating the gate, and understanding boarding/closure times. It feeds naturally into 013 (boarding groups), 014 (gate changes), 015 (gate-check of cabin bags) and 016 (missed the gate).

## 1. Research question

What does a traveller need to know and do to find their gate once they are through security, which parts of "the gate" belong in Scene 012 rather than Scenes 013–016, and what should be trained as stable language vs operator-specific timing?

The lesson should train the smallest complete task:

> Leave security → check the departure screen for flight number / gate / status → confirm gate and walk time → go to the gate (following signs / a map) → arrive with enough time left → be ready for the boarding call.

Scene 012 should NOT teach boarding-group order (013), responding to a gate change (014), bag gate-checks (015), or the missed-gate recovery (016).

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Departure screens / the gate number appears

At UK airports the **flight information screens** in the departure lounge show each flight with its **flight number, destination, scheduled time, gate and status** (e.g. `Boarding`, `Last Call`, `Gate closed`). Heathrow states: "**Your departure gate is usually shown on the flight information screens around 45 minutes before departure.** Check the screens regularly and leave enough time to get to your gate."

BA at Heathrow Terminal 5 says gate information "**becomes available shortly before the flight opens**" and advises passengers to "check the information screens at regular intervals" after clearing security. B/C gates may take about **20 minutes** to reach, so BA says be sure the flight is on the screens before leaving the main terminal. Gate numbers can change (→ Scene 014).

Real-world learner tasks:

- find a departure screen and read the row for their flight;
- match flight number (not just destination);
- note the gate letter/number and the status word;
- confirm the **walk time** (large airports, satellite terminals, trains);
- go to the gate and arrive by the boarding/closing time.

### 2.2 Boarding time vs gate-closing time — a key distinction

Two different moments matter:

- **Boarding time** — when boarding is scheduled to start (passengers are called group by group; see 013).
- **Gate closing / boarding closes** — the cut-off by which you must be at the gate; after this the aircraft door may close and you can be left behind.

Airlines publish these per flight (on the boarding pass/app and gate board). BA's Heathrow T5 page gives example targets ("be at the boarding gate at least 20 minutes before departure") — these are airline/airport examples, **not** universal.

Course rule: teach the **concept and vocabulary** (boarding time, gate closes, be at the gate by, final call), and never present one airline's cutoff minutes as a universal rule.

### 2.3 Hearing the calls

Besides the screens, the lounge plays announcements: `Now boarding`, `Final/Last boarding call`, `Passengers for flight ___` (→ also Scene 014 for a change, and 016 for a missed call). Use the app/boarding pass as extra confirmation (BA app sends gate notifications).

### 2.4 Finding the gate physically

Follow **signage** (gate letters/numbers), **interactive maps / wayfinding** (Heathrow and BA offer maps and QR-code wayfinding), or a **navigation app / airport app**. At big airports a **transit/train** may be needed for satellite piers.

### 2.5 End of the task

Success = arrive at the gate in time, then the gate agent scans the boarding pass at boarding (Scene 013).

## 3. Real-world English

### 3.1 Signs / screens / documents

- Departures / Flight information / Departure lounge
- Gate B27 / A12 / C (letter + number)
- Flight number (e.g. BA 123)
- Boarding / Now boarding / Final call / Last call / Gate closed
- On time / Delayed / Cancelled
- Scheduled time / Departure time / Boarding time / Gate closes
- Wayfinding / Map / Transit train

### 3.2 High-probability staff announcements / intentions

- `Now boarding flight ___ at gate ___.`
- `Flight BA123 is now boarding at Gate 15.`
- `Last / final call for passengers on flight ___ .`
- `Gate ___ will close in ten minutes.`
- `Please proceed to Gate ___.`
- `Passengers requiring assistance may board first.` (→ 013 pre-boarding)

Learner questions/statements:

- `Which gate is my flight departing from?`
- `Where can I find my gate?`
- `How long does it take to get to Gate C?`
- `What time does boarding start?`
- `What time does the gate close?`
- `Is my flight still on Gate 12?` (→ checks for change, Scene 014)
- `Where is the departure screen?` / `Which screen is my flight on?`

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Matching the wrong flight / the wrong destination

High-risk: same city/airport, changed flight numbers. Train matching by **flight number + destination + time**, not destination alone.

### 4.2 Confusing boarding time with departure time and the closure time

Real and common. Train the three separate numbers and their vocabulary.

### 4.3 Underestimating walk time at a large airport / satellite piers

Real: B/C gates ~20 min at LHR; gates can be far. Train `How long does it take to get to ___?`.

### 4.4 Assuming a printed/app gate is final — not checking the screens

Gate numbers can change (Scene 014) and screen statuses update. Train "check the screens regularly."

### 4.5 Not recognising the status words (Boarding / Last call / Gate closed)

Train the recognition of `Now boarding`, `Final call`, `Gate closed` and what they mean for how fast to move.

## 5. Current rules / policy-sensitive facts

`policy_sensitive: true`

`last_verified: 2026-08`

- A gate is usually shown around **45 minutes before departure** at Heathrow, and **becomes available shortly before the flight opens** (BA LHR T5); this is airport/airline guidance and varies.
- **Boarding time and gate-closing time are different**; the cut-off by which you must be at the gate is set by the airline per flight. BA's example ("be at the gate at least 20 minutes before departure"; pre-boarding families 50 min) are **examples, not universal**.
- Gate numbers **can change** (a theme for Scene 014); check the screens and gate announcements.
- Walk times can be long and satellites need a **transit train** (e.g. LHR B/C ~20 min) — arrive with buffer.
- Status words (`Now boarding`, `Last call`, `Gate closed`) appear on screens and in announcements at all airports but the exact wording varies.

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 012

- reading a departure screen row (flight number–destination–time–gate–status);
- distinguishing boarding time vs departure time vs gate-closing time;
- asking for/confirming the gate and walk time;
- using app/map/wayfinding and transit for satellite gates;
- recognising `Now boarding`, `Final call`, `Gate closed`;
- checking screens regularly because gates can change (handoff to 014).

### Move out of Scene 012

- boarding-group order and scanning → Scene 013;
- responding to a gate change → Scene 014;
- gate-check of cabin baggage → Scene 015;
- missed-the-gate recovery → Scene 016.

### Reusable patterns (PATTERN_BANK.md)

- P055 — `Which gate / desk / lane do I need?`
- P056 — `Has + noun + been called yet?`
- P057 — `Is this the right + noun + for me?`
- Repair patterns (universal section).

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

To keep Scene 012 useful it will NOT train:

- one universal arrival/boarding/closure time (varies by airline/airport/route);
- boarding-group order (013);
- responding to gate changes (014);
- claiming denied-boarding rights (016);
- mapping the entire terminal (directional signs are teachable vocabulary, not memorised routes).

The learner should instead leave Scene 012 able to:

1. find their flight on the departure screen and read gate/time/status;
2. understand and state the differences between boarding time, departure time and gate-closing time;
3. confirm the gate and the walk time (`Which gate? How long does it take?`);
4. move to the gate with a buffer and recognise `Now boarding` / `Last call` / `Gate closed`;
5. keep checking screens because the gate may change.
