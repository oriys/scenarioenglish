# Research — Scene 015 Gate-checking cabin baggage

> A distinct and increasingly common boarding moment: a passenger's **cabin (hand) bag** ends up going in the **hold** at the gate instead of into the overhead bin. The learner must understand why this happens, what to do when gate staff ask for the bag, and — critically — what they must **take out** of the bag first because batteries/power banks may not go in the hold.

## 1. Research question

Why and when does an airline "gate-checks" a cabin bag, what does the traveller see/do, and what safety-critical packing rule changes the task from a simple "sure" into a more careful exchange?

The lesson should train the smallest complete task:

> At the gate (or aircraft door) staff say a cabin bag must go in the hold → remove the small under-seat bag with valuables/batteries/medicines if needed → hand the larger bag over (and it gets tagged) → keep essentials with you → board, bag travels in the hold.

Scene 015 is about **handing over a bag at the gate correctly and safely.** It is not about luggage fees in general (003) or liquids/electronics at security (007).

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Why a gate-check happens

Airlines gate-check a cabin bag when:

- **overhead-bin space runs out** (full flight / late boarders) — airlines are allowed to ask because the bag can't be boarded as a carry-on due to space;
- the bag is **too large** for the cabin fittings at boarding time (spot-check before the aircraft door);
- the **aircraft size/cabin layout** means less bin space than expected.

This is written into conditions of carriage (e.g. Ryanair requires passengers to leave bags "at the aircraft steps, in the gate bag trolley, or as directed… for stowage in the hold" when cabin space isn't available).

### 2.2 The two "where" options

- **At the gate / departure-lounge podium:** staff check the bag before you board; it is labelled and sent down to the hold. You hand it over, keep your essentials, and board empty-handed.
- **At / on the aircraft steps or jet-bridge ("aircraft-side"):** on some flights the bag is taken at the boarding steps/trolley and placed in the hold near the aircraft.

Either way the bag is treated like checked baggage for that flight (tagged/identified), and it is **not charged** as an extra bag — it is your own cabin allowance being moved, not extra baggage.

### 2.3 The safety-critical packing rule (batteries/power banks)

This is the key current fact that turns Scene 015 into real instruction, not just "sure":

- **CAA dangerous-goods rules:** **spare lithium batteries and power banks must not be placed in checked (hold) baggage** — they must be carried in the cabin (carry-on). A power bank may only be carried as **carry-on** baggage (max 2 power banks, ≤100Wh without approval).
- **BA states it directly:** power banks and spare batteries are **forbidden in checked baggage** and must be carried in hand baggage; and "all spare device batteries and power packs must be carried into the cabin in your **smaller under-seat item** of hand baggage, **in case your larger item needs to be checked into the hold**."

So when a larger cabin bag is gate-checked, the traveller must first **move spare batteries and the power bank (and ideally valuables/electronics/medicines) into the smaller under-seat hand bag**. This is the practical, genuinely useful content.

### 2.4 Extra / oversized bags are a different case

Scene 015 is about the bag where the passenger already has the **right** to bring it as cabin baggage but it gets sent to the hold. A bag that simply **exceeds the cabin allowance** is a baggage-fee matter (Scene 003) — they are different tasks.

### 2.5 End of the task

Success = essentials kept with you, larger bag tagged and sent to the hold, you board and collect it at your destination with the other checked bags.

## 3. Real-world English

### 3.1 Signs / staff wording / tags

- `Cabin bags may need to go in the hold`
- `The overhead bins are full — may we check your bag?`
- `We'll need to place your bag in the hold.`
- `Please take out your valuables and any batteries.`
- `Do you have a smaller bag to keep with you?`
- **Tag wording/vocabulary:** check tag / label / "gate check" / baggage tag / red or coloured tag (airline-specific), baggage identification.

### 3.2 Staff intentions

1. tell the passenger the bag must go in the hold (space/size);
2. ask to take essentials/batteries/power bank out first;
3. tag the bag and give a receipt/identification if applicable;
4. direct the passenger to board.

Representative natural wording:

- `I'm sorry, the cabin is full — your bag will need to go in the hold.`
- `Could we check this cabin bag, please?`
- `Before I take it, please remove anything you need and any spare batteries or power banks.`
- `Keep your valuables and medicines with you.`
- `Here's your tag — your bag will come back with the other checked bags.`
- `You can board now — we've got your bag.`

Learner questions/statements:

- `No problem — do you need it now?`
- `Can I take a few things out first?`
- `Where will I get my bag back?`
- `Do I get it back at the aircraft door or at arrivals?`
- `Is it free?` / `Will I be charged?`
- `I have a power bank in here — let me take it out first.`

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Leaving a power bank / spare batteries in the gate-checked bag

**The critical, real failure** (CAA + BA: not permitted in the hold). Train the automatic response: move power bank + spare batteries (and valuables/medicines/electronics) into the under-seat bag.

### 4.2 Not removing valuables / electronics / medicines

BA notes a cabin bag "may contain no valuables, electronics or essential medicines" precisely because it may go in the hold. Train the take-out habit.

### 4.3 Assuming it's a fee / refusing and causing delay

Gate-checks of your own cabin allowance are generally **not charged**; refusing to cooperate can hold up boarding. Teach calm cooperation (`No problem — sure`).

### 4.4 Not knowing where the bag will return (door vs arrivals)

Differs by airline/aircraft/airport. Teach asking `Where do I collect it?` rather than assuming.

### 4.5 Confusing the two "gate-check" triggers

- bag that's fully within allowance but too big for the cabin / bins full → Scene 015 (no fee);
- bag beyond the allowance / oversized from the start → Scene 003 (fees).
Train the distinction.

## 5. Current rules / policy-sensitive facts

`policy_sensitive: true`

`last_verified: 2026-08`

- A **cabin back may be gate-checked** (sent to the hold) when overhead space is short or the bag can't be fitted at boarding; this is in airline conditions of carriage (e.g. Ryanair's "leave bags at the aircraft steps / gate bag trolley … for stowage in the hold"). Gate-checking your own cabin allowance is **not an extra-bag fee** in the normal case, but confirm with the airline.
- **Safety-critical (stable, regulator-backed):** **spare lithium batteries and power banks are not permitted in hold baggage**; they must travel in the cabin. CAA: power banks are carry-on only, max **2 power banks per person**, up to **100 Wh without airline approval** (100–160 Wh needs approval). BA: power banks/spare batteries must be in your hand baggage, in the smaller **under-seat item**, "in case your larger item needs to be checked into the hold."
- Baggage allowances and exact cabin-bag sizes (e.g. BA cabin bag **56 × 45 × 25 cm**, under-seat hand bag **40 × 30 × 15 cm**) are **airline-specific and change** — do not teach as universal. (Examples for role-play only.)
- Where a gate-checked bag is reclaimed (at the aircraft door vs the arrivals carousel) varies by airline/aircraft; ask rather than assume.
- Oversized/beyond-allowance bags are a **fee** matter (Scene 003), a different task.

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 015

- recognising the request to gate-check a cabin bag and cooperating calmly;
- the must-do **take-out** habit: power bank, spare batteries, valuables, electronics, essential medicines → small under-seat bag;
- reading a gate-check tag and asking where the bag is reclaimed;
- the fee question (`Will I be charged?`) with the honest "usually free for your own cabin allowance — confirm";
- distinguishing a gate-check (allowance OK, space) from an oversized/fee case (003);
- repair/confirm phrases.

### Move out of Scene 015

- overweight/oversize fees in general → Scene 003;
- liquids/electronics at security screening → Scene 007;
- boarding groups/scanning → Scene 013;
- missed-boarding/rebooking → Scene 016;
- losing the gate-checked bag / damage claims → delayed-lost baggage (017/023 and baggage claims, not here).

### Reusable patterns (PATTERN_BANK.md)

- P003 — `Could you + action, please?`
- P050 — `Am I allowed to + action?` (e.g. keep my power bank?)
- P053 — `Do I need to take + noun + out?`
- P063 — `What happens if + 情况?` (bag in the hold)
- P067 — `Will I be + past participle?` (charged?)
- Repair phrases.

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

To keep Scene 015 accurate it will NOT train:

- a fixed cabin-bag size/weight as universal (airline-specific, changes);
- full watt-hour/battery engineering detail (007/001 boundaries; just "take the power bank out");
- all dangerous-goods categories;
- bag fee tables (003);
- a claim that gate-checking is always free or always tagged (confirm first).

The learner should instead leave Scene 015 able to:

1. recognise when and why a cabin bag is gate-checked;
2. calmly agree and **take out the power bank, spare batteries, valuables, electronics and medicines**;
3. hand the bag over and keep the essentials in the small under-seat bag;
4. ask where the bag will be reclaimed and whether there's any charge;
5. tell a gate-check apart from an oversized/fee case.
