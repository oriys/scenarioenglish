## 1. Research question

Which overweight / oversized baggage rules does a UK traveller most need to get right, and how far should Scene 003 go?

Specific points to verify against official sources:

1. British Airways standard economy checked-bag weight limit (kg) and the cap above which a bag cannot be checked.
2. How British Airways charges excess — per kg, per piece, or flat/price-banded? (This determines whether "repack / take kilos out" actually saves money.)
3. Where/how excess is paid — online vs airport, and whether there is a separate payment desk.
4. The outsize / oversized baggage drop-off process and the maximum dimensions BA will take.
5. The separate hand-baggage (cabin bag) limits, because repacking only helps if the hand bag is not itself over.

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Standard economy checked bag

British Airways ("baggage essentials", by-fare allowances):

- **Economy standard checked bag: weight up to 23 kg (50 lb) per bag.**
- **Maximum dimensions of a standard checked bag: 90 x 75 x 43 cm (35 x 30 x 17 in).**
- The 23 kg limit applies **per bag** and "can't be split between multiple bags"; BA also explicitly states you **cannot pool, share or combine your checked-baggage allowance** with another traveller, even in the same party.

Course implication: Scene 003's `23 kg per bag, not a total` and the "合并计重 (pooling) 不被允许" abnormal are both supported. The pooling point is a genuine BA rule, not only a hypothetical.

### 2.2 Where the line is drawn: the heavy bag charge

BA FAQ ("What should I do if my bag weighs over 23 kg?"):

- If a checked bag weighs more than the allowance, a **heavy bag charge** applies.
- **No checked bag may weigh over 32 kg.**
- The heavy bag charge applies to a bag **between 23 kg and 32 kg that is over the allowance**.
- **Charge: £65 / €75 / $100 / C$100 per overweight bag, per one-way journey**, converted to local currency where needed; some destinations add local taxes.

### 2.3 How BA charges excess — IMPORTANT (per bag, not per kg for BA)

British Airways does **not** typically charge excess by per-kilogram. BA's model is two flat, per-bag charges:

1. **Heavy bag charge** — for a bag over 23 kg (up to 32 kg) that is over the allowance: flat £65/€75/$100/C$100 per bag.
2. **Extra bag charge** — for each additional piece beyond the allowance: price depends on destination and how many extra bags; you can add **up to 10 extra checked bags**.

Course implication: Scene 003's working assumption "超重费按件按公斤收" is true as a *general market* description, but for **BA specifically it is per bag / flat**, not per kg. This is a high-value distinction to teach: with BA, taking kilos out of a 25 kg bag down to 23 kg does **not** necessarily reduce the charge to zero unless the bag then sits under the allowance — the flat heavy-bag fee is avoided only by getting back under the allowance. The scene's "is that per kilo or per bag?" pattern is exactly the right question to train.

### 2.4 Repacking as the cheapest option

Because the charge is flat per bag over the allowance (and per extra piece), moving weight into the hand bag to get under the 23 kg limit is often the cheapest fix — this supports Scene 003's "repack first" guidance, provided the hand bag itself stays within its own limit (see 2.6).

### 2.5 Payment: online vs airport, and the payment point

BA FAQ ("Can I take extra checked baggage?"):

- You get the **best price by adding extra bags online via Manage My Booking before check-in** — up to 10 extra bags at a **discounted online rate** (card, Avios, or both).
- **After checking in, extra bags can only be added at the airport at the airport rate**, and Avios can no longer be used.
- **At many airports, including London Heathrow, London Gatwick and in North America, you can only pay using a credit or debit card. Cash is not accepted; Avios cannot be used** for excess/heavy-bag payment at the airport.

Course implication: Scene 003's "线上更便宜" and "柜台普遍不收现金、只收卡" are both supported for BA. About the **separate payment desk**: BA's official text describes paying at the airport at the airport rate, and it confirms card-only at major UK airports, but it does **not** state on its own that a separate physical payment desk is always used. The "you must pay at a payment desk and come back" model is a common UK experience but is not a universal BA rule — treat it as a flow variant to teach as a possibility (the scene already frames it as "常见"), not as guaranteed. Note also that BA explicitly says refunds for pre-paid extra bags are made to the original card.

### 2.6 Separate hand-baggage limits (cabin)

British Airways economy cabin allowance is separate and fixed independently of checked baggage:

- **Hand bag (under the seat): max 40 x 30 x 15 cm** (guaranteed in the cabin; carry essentials).
- **Cabin bag: max 56 x 45 x 25 cm** (must go in the overhead bin; may need to go in the hold).
- (Some fares/routes list a slightly larger hand-bag size 45 x 36 x 20 cm — always check the fare.)

Course implication: Scene 003's warning that "随身行李有独立限制，转移前先确认" is correct. Moving excess into the cabin bag only works if the cabin bag stays within its own size/weight limit.

### 2.7 Oversized / outsize baggage

British Airways baggage essentials:

- Standard checked bag max 90 x 75 x 43 cm.
- **Maximum dimensions for an oversize bag in the hold: 190 x 75 x 65 cm.** A bag larger than the standard size but within these maximum dimensions is treated as an oversize bag (not rejected, but handled specially).
- Sports equipment (e.g. skis/snowboards) within **190 x 75 x 65 cm** can be carried as part of the checked-baggage allowance.
- Items exceeding the maximum dimensions typically require **prior airline approval** and are handled at a dedicated channel/desk, not the normal belt.

Course implication: Scene 003 is right that oversize/outsize items go to a dedicated channel rather than the normal belt. The 90 x 75 x 43 cm standard and 190 x 75 x 65 cm maximum are useful, BA-specific figures for the note; they change by airline, so mark them as examples.

## 3. Real-world English

- `baggage allowance` — the free amount on your ticket.
- `checked baggage` / `checked bag` / `hold luggage`.
- `hand baggage` / `cabin bag` / `hand bag`.
- `overweight`; `it's over` / `a bit over`.
- `oversized` / `outsize` (UK signage often uses `outsize`).
- `excess baggage`; `excess baggage charges apply`.
- `heavy bag charge` — BA's official name for the over-23kg fee.
- `extra bag charge` — BA's official name for an additional piece.
- `per kilo or per bag?` — the key clarification question.
- `payment desk` — a common UK routing for excess payment (variant, not universal).
- `card only` / `no cash` / `contactless`.

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Assuming excess is always per kilogram

For BA it is a flat heavy-bag charge per bag, not per kg; for other airlines it can differ. Train the learner to ask `Is that per kilo or per bag?` and to reason about whether removing kilos actually helps.

### 4.2 Confusing overweight with oversized

`overweight` (fix by removing weight) vs `oversized` (fix by channel/approval; removing weight does nothing). Scene 003 already teaches this distinction and it is correct.

### 4.3 Assuming the airline accepts any weight

BA caps a checked bag at 32 kg — above that the bag cannot be checked as presented. Never implied that any weight can be paid for.

### 4.4 Not knowing the online discount exists

Extra/heavy bags are cheaper bought online via Manage My Booking before check-in; at the airport you pay the airport rate. A learner who just pays at the desk may overpay. Scene 003's "线上更便宜" is supported.

### 4.5 Being surprised by card-only

At Heathrow/Gatwick/North America, cash is not accepted for excess baggage. The card-only abnormal in the scene is real and important.

### 4.6 Moving excess into a hand bag that is itself over

The cabin bag has its own limit; repacking must respect both limits.

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 003

- understanding `overweight` vs `oversized`;
- asking the excess amount and the charging unit (`per kilo or per bag?`);
- the repack-first economic move (take kilos out / move to hand bag), with the caveat on the cabin bag's own limit;
- online-vs-airport price difference (cheaper online, no Avios/cash at airport);
- the card-only reality;
- the separate outsize/oversized channel;
- the "pay at the payment desk and come back" possibility (as a variant, not a promise);
- polite bad-news language (`a bit over`, `I'm afraid`) and number checks.

### Move out of Scene 003

- **extend the limit detail for oversized**: keep the doorway to "oversize goes to a dedicated channel", but push the full dimensions / sports-equipment / prior-approval rules to an extended note (Scene 015 or sports-equipment material) — Scene 003 should not teach the 190 x 75 x 65 figures as content;
- **seat fees / seat economics** → Scene 004;
- **gate-check of cabin baggage** → Scene 015;
- **through-checking / connection baggage fees** → Scene 005.

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

- a single "the" heavy-bag price or exact figure that would read as universal;
- all possible per-kg models of other airlines (BA is flat per bag; others differ — warn, don't encyclopaedise);
- the full outsize/sports-equipment sizing and approval procedure;
- pooling negotiation (BA disallows it — treat "we can't pool" as a realistic rejection to hear, not something to fight);
- dangerous-goods thresholds beyond the power-bank carry-on note already carried from Scene 001.

Scene 003 should leave a learner able to: hear and confirm the overweight/oversize conclusion and figures; ask the charge basis and options; choose repack vs pay (and where to pay, online vs airport / card only); follow an oversized-bag routing; and complete the transaction politely with receipts and number checks.

## 9. Conflicts with the current scene body (for the author)

- Scene 003's framing "超重费按件按公斤收" is true as a market-wide generalisation but misdescribes **British Airways, which charges a flat heavy-bag fee per bag (not per kg)**. If BA is the reference carrier, re-word to teach the `per kilo or per bag?` question and, for BA, the flat per-bag model — otherwise the Repack-vs-pay example economics (Scene 003 Role Play B: "second bag £45 vs excess £60") are internally consistent but rest on an assumption that is not BA's published per-kg method.
- Scene 003 Role Play B prices (`second bag £45`, `60 pounds for the extra weight`) are fictional and that is fine — but ensure they are marked fictional and not presented as current BA prices. BA publishes the heavy bag charge as £65 (2026-08) and said price changes.
- The "独立 payment desk、柜台人员本身不收款" claim is a common UK experience but not a published, guaranteed BA fact — soften to "you may be directed to a payment desk".
- The claim "磅秤读数就是最终依据，不接受在家称的说法" is a reasonable operational description but should be phrased as practice, not a quoted rule.
