# Research — Scene 011 Answering border questions

> This note covers the **second of the two "departure control" scenes (01.03)**: when a Border Force officer at the manual passport-control desk asks the questions. The learner's job is to understand the **intent** of each question (purpose / duration / accommodation / funds / employment / return), answer in short, truthful, natural English, and offer evidence if asked. Scene 010 owns reaching the desk and the eGate referral; Scene 011 owns the Q&A itself.

## 1. Research question

What does a traveller actually need to hear and say when a UK Border Force officer asks questions at passport control, and which of those questions is it realistic and useful to train at A2–B1?

The lesson should train the smallest complete task:

> Approach the desk → hand over passport → understand and answer the officer's questions (purpose, length of stay, accommodation, funds, work, return) → provide evidence if asked → be cleared to enter.

Scene 011 is about **answering border questions truthfully and briefly.** It does NOT train eGate eligibility (010), prohibited goods/customs, or visa-application procedures.

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Who gets asked (and why it matters for the scene)

GOV.UK (Entering the UK) says a traveller's passport "will be checked at border control" and that non-EU visitors "will usually be asked why you're coming to the UK." British citizens and eligible eGate users may pass through without an interview, but many visitors — and anyone referred from an eGate — face desk questions. GOV.UK's Standard Visitor page is explicit: "If you do not need a visa, you must still meet the [Standard Visitor] eligibility requirements. **You may be asked questions at the UK border** about your eligibility and the activities you plan to do."

Course implication: this is a real, common exchange for visitors; train it even though happy-path eGate users may not hit it.

### 2.2 What the officer is checking (the eligibility skeleton)

Under the Standard Visitor framework a visitor must be able to show that they:

- will **leave the UK at the end of the visit**;
- can **support themselves (and dependants)** during the trip, or are funded by someone else;
- can **pay for their return or onward journey**, or are funded by someone;
- will not live in the UK long-term through frequent visits / make it their main home.

From this, the officer's questions follow a predictable predicted pattern: **purpose → duration → accommodation → funds → work/ties → return**.

### 2.3 Evidence the traveller may be asked to show

Immigration-law guidance (e.g. DavidsonMorris) and university arrival advice (e.g. QMUL) note officers may ask for evidence of:

- **accommodation** (where you're staying — hotel confirmation, invitation/letter);
- **return travel** (return/onward ticket);
- **sufficient funds** (bank statement / sponsor's evidence);
- work/study or family ties that pull the traveller home.

QMUL's arrival page lists for students: valid passport, eVisa print-out + share code, decision letter, travel ticket, and evidence of enough money for fees and accommodation. (Not all of this is asked of a standard visitor; it's the "be prepared to show" set.)

### 2.4 The exchange

A typical manual desk flow: officer greets, asks for the passport (/travel document), asks the visit questions, possibly asks for an evidence document, then clears or asks more. Success = the traveller is admitted.

## 3. Real-world English

### 3.1 High-probability officer questions (intent families)

1. Purpose — `What is the purpose of your visit? / Why have you come to the UK?`
2. Activity — `What are you planning to do while you're here?`
3. Duration — `How long are you staying? / When are you leaving?`
4. Accommodation — `Where are you staying? / Where is your accommodation?`
5. Work/ties — `What do you do for a living? / What is your job? / Do you work?`
6. Funds — `Can you support yourself? / How will you fund your trip?`
7. Return — `Do you have a return ticket? / When do you plan to go home?`
8. Relationship (with children) — `What is your relationship to the child?`

### 3.2 Learner output (short, truthful, natural)

- `I'm here on holiday. / I'm visiting my family. / I'm here for a short course.`
- `For two weeks. / I'm staying for a week.`
- `I'm staying at the Grand Hotel. / I'm staying with my sister in London.`
- `I'm a teacher / I work in a bank / I'm a student.`
- `Yes, I can support myself. / I have money for the trip.`
- `Yes, I have a return ticket. / I'm flying back on the 12th.`
- `I'm his mother / her father.` (child relationship)

### 3.3 Evidence-request handling

- `Do you have proof of your accommodation? / Can you show me your hotel booking?`
- `May I see your return ticket?`
- Learner: `Of course — here you are. / Here's my hotel booking. / It's on my phone, one moment.`

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Giving wrong/long/memorised answers

The most important real-world rule (echoed widely, and a core Standard Visitor principle) is **be truthful and keep it simple** — do not memorise "fake" answers. A short, consistent, honest answer beats a rehearsed story.

### 4.2 Not understanding the question — freezing

Officers rephrase or simplify; the learner needs repair phrases rather than silence.

- `Sorry, could you say that again?`
- `Sorry, what does "purpose of visit" mean?`
- `Could you speak a little more slowly, please?`

### 4.3 Not being able to state dates/places/number names

Numbers, dates, and place names are high-risk listening. Train: `for two weeks`, `on the 12th of August`, `the Grand Hotel`, `for a week`.

### 4.4 Confusing "stay" (duration) with "where you're staying" (accommodation)

Both are asked; teach the meaning and both answer shapes.

### 4.5 Not having evidence ready when asked

Encourage carrying mobile/paper evidence (booking, return ticket, funds) and the phrase to offer it.

## 5. Current rules / policy-sensitive facts

`policy_sensitive: true`

`last_verified: 2026-08`

- Officers may ask "simple questions about your stay" (GOV.UK; QMUL). The question set tracks the Standard Visitor eligibility criteria: purpose, duration, accommodation, funds, work/ties, return.
- A visitor may be asked for evidence of **accommodation, return travel and sufficient funds** (corroborated by immigration-law guidance); a student per university guidance is expected to carry visa/status/funding evidence.
- Visits are capped at **up to 6 months** for a Standard Visitor; some nationalities need a **visa**, some need an **ETA** (£20, Heathrow), and some need neither — but they all must still meet eligibility and may be asked questions.
- **Do not teach a fixed list of "the questions."** The phrasing varies by officer/situation; teach intent families and truthful short answers.
- Border officers also sometimes ask questions to **safeguarding** when an adult travels with a child of a different surname (see eGate reasons and family guidance) — the traveller should state the relationship and be ready to show relationship evidence.

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 011

- understanding the intent behind the 6–7 question families;
- short, truthful answers (purpose, duration, accommodation, funds, work, return);
- handling a child-relationship question;
- offering/reading evidence (hotel booking, return ticket, bank evidence);
- date/number/place-name listening;
- repair phrases for not hearing/understanding.

### Move out of Scene 011

- eGate eligibility and referral → Scene 010;
- customs/prohibited-goods → later / not here;
- visa application steps → not taught (mention "you may need a visa/ETA" only).

### Reusable patterns (PATTERN_BANK.md)

- P016 — `Did you say + information?` (repair)
- P018 — `So, just to confirm…` (confirming).
- P063 — `What happens if + 情况?`
- Universal repair phrases (could you say that again / more slowly; could you write it down; what does ___ mean).

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

To keep Scene 011 accurate and useful it will NOT train:

- a fixed/official "list of border questions" (phrasing varies; we teach intent families);
- visa application or ETA application mechanics;
- long, rehearsed "interview" speeches (the real advice is short and truthful);
- demonstrating detailed financial/travel documentation rules for every nationality group.

The learner should instead leave Scene 011 able to:

1. understand the purpose and key phrases of the visit questions;
2. answer briefly, truthfully and naturally (purpose, length, accommodation, funds, work, return);
3. ask for repetition/clarification without freezing;
4. offer evidence (booking, return ticket) when asked;
5. state relationship when travelling with a child of a different surname.
