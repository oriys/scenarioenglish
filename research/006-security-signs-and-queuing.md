# Research — Scene 006 Security Signage & Queuing

> This note covers the first encounter with airport security: reading the signs, understanding queuing / lane vocabulary, and what the learner must understand while waiting in line. It is the first of four scenes on the security sub-theme (006–009).

## 1. Research question

What does a traveller actually need to understand and do when they first reach the security screening area — before they have to deal with liquids, electronics, bag searches or confiscation — and which parts belong in Scene 006 rather than Scenes 007–009?

The lesson should train the smallest complete task:

> Leave check-in → find the correct security lane/queue → arrive prepared (documents, pockets, coat) → wait/queue in the right line → follow the officer's instructions to the screening point → pass through and collect belongings → continue to the departure lounge.

Scene 006 should NOT teach liquids/electronics detail (007), the secondary bag search (008), or prohibited-item confiscation (009). Those need their own scenes.

## 2. Real-world flow

### 2.1 Finding the security area / the queue

At UK airports the security checkpoint is usually signposted after check-in and bag drop, with overhead signs reading `Security`, `Departures` or `All Passengers`. Travellers join a queue (called a **queue** in UK English, variously a **lane** or **line** in US English).

Real-world learner tasks:

- find the correct security checkpoint / lane;
- join the right queue (normal, Fast Track / Express Lane, or cabin-bag-only where offered);
- have any required document/boarding pass ready if the airport asks (Gatwick explicitly says a passport is NOT required at security and only the boarding pass needs to be scanned; other airports vary);
- prepare the tray items while in the queue so they don't hold people up.

### 2.2 Allow time / how to get ready

Heathrow's official advice for arrival times:

- international / long-haul flights: arrive about **3 hours before** departure;
- short-haul / European flights: arrive about **2 hours before** departure.

These are airport recommendations, not fixed rules — check-in closes at airline-set times, and queues vary by time of day and terminal.

Heathrow's "Prepare for security" checklist (terminals 2–5):

- leave liquids in your cabin bag (containers up to 2L);
- leave laptops/tablets/other large electronics in your bag;
- place coat/jacket in a tray if asked;
- empty pockets (keys, coins, phone) into a tray;
- follow the security officer's instructions;
- empty reusable metal or insulated bottles before security (refill afterwards);
- have all electronic devices charged — a device that cannot be switched on may be refused.

### 2.3 The screening steps

The typical order at a UK airport checkspoint:

1. queue at the checkpoint;
2. when called forward, put cabin bags / coat / loose items in trays;
3. walk through the body scanner (or magnetometer);
4. collect trays on the other side;
5. replace belongings in bags and move away from the belt to avoid blocking the queue.

Some airports operate **Fast Track** (paid express lane) and/or separate **cabin-bag-only** arrangements; availability and price vary by airport.

### 2.4 End of the queueing task

Once through the security point, the traveller is in the departure lounge. Gate-finding and boarding are later scenes (Scene 012 / 013).

Course implication: Scene 006 should end at the handoff "you're through security," not move into gate-finding or boarding.

## 3. Real-world English

### 3.1 Signs / labels

- Security
- Departures
- All Passengers / Passengers Only
- Empty your pockets
- Place bags in trays
- Fast Track / Express Lane
- Liquids / Electronic devices (where separate signs still exist)
- Queue this way / Please queue here

### 3.2 Verbs / process vocabulary

- queue / join the queue
- lane / line (UK vs US)
- tray / bin / tub
- empty your pockets
- step through
- body scanner / security scanner / metal detector
- screening / search
- boarding pass / passport (which one is needed varies)

### 3.3 High-probability staff instruction intentions

UK security officers use short, direct instructions. The lesson should teach intent families and common phrasings:

1. direct you to a lane / queue;
2. ask you to put bags/coats in a tray;
3. ask you to empty pockets / remove belt or shoes;
4. tell you to step through the scanner;
5. tell you when you're clear to collect items;
6. warn that a bag will need extra screening.

Representative natural wording:

- `Please join the queue on your left.`
- `This lane, please.`
- `Take off your coat and put it in the tray.`
- `Empty your pockets into the tray, please.`
- `Have your boarding pass ready, please.`
- `Step through when you're ready.`
- `You're clear — you can collect your items.`
- `Your bag needs a secondary search / an additional check.`
- `Please wait while our colleague checks your bag.`

## 4. Common failure points

### 4.1 Missing a document or not being "ready" at the scanner

Different airports ask for different things at security (boarding pass vs nothing at all). This is worth training as "listen to the instruction," not as a universal rule.

### 4.2 Joining the wrong queue / lane

Learners may default to the first queue without noticing Fast Track or a cabin-bag-only lane, or may not realise some lanes are staffed vs self. Trained language: confirming which queue and asking if they're in the right place.

### 4.3 Blocking the belt / holding up the queue

Not preparing pockets/trays in advance slows everyone. This is a natural, real communicative failure with real English — worth training as "get ready before you reach the belt."

### 4.4 Time pressure

If a learner hasn't left enough time, queuing can feel stressful. Scene 006 should reference the 2h/3h recommendation but never publish it as a universal cutoff.

## 5. Current rules / policy-sensitive facts

`policy_sensitive: true`

`last_verified: 2026-08`

- Security lanes/queues are normal flow; **Fast Track** availability and price differ by airport (Heathrow, Stansted, etc.).
- Arrival-time guidance is airport-specific, not law: Heathrow recommends ~2h (short-haul) / ~3h (long-haul) before departure. Check-in closes at airline-set times.
- At security the passenger typically shows a **boarding pass** (or nothing) depending on the airport; **do not teach "passport required at security"** as universal (Gatwick explicitly says it is not required).
- Trays, coat removal, pocket-emptying and device-charging rules vary; the officer's live instruction is authoritative.
- Secondary / manual bag checks are becoming more common even at airports with new CT scanners (Which?, Jan 2026), so Scene 006 should acknowledge an "extra check" can happen to anyone.

## 7. Course design decisions

### Keep in Scene 006

- finding the security checkpoint and joining the right queue / lane;
- queueing vocabulary (`queue`, `lane`, `line`, `tray`) and the difference UK `queue` vs US `line`;
- getting ready (coat, pockets, trays, charged devices);
- following the officer's short instructions (queue → tray → scanner → clear → collect);
- one lightweight "am I in the right queue / lane" confirmation;
- acknowledging an "additional check" may happen (feeds into 008);
- leaving enough time (teach the idea, not a universal number).

### Move out of Scene 006

- liquids-in-bag detail → Scene 007;
- electronics / laptop in-or-out detail → Scene 007;
- secondary / manual bag search language → Scene 008;
- prohibited items and confiscation → Scene 009;
- gate finding → Scene 012;
- boarding → Scene 013.

### Reusable patterns to reference in the scene body (PATTERN_BANK.md)

- P054 — `Am I in the right queue for + noun?` (K. queue/sequence)
- P055 — `Which gate / desk / lane do I need?` (K.)
- P057 — `Is this the right + noun + for me?` (K.)
- P053 — `Do I need to take + noun + out?` (J., pre-links to 007)
- P003 — `Could you + action, please?`
- P050 — `Am I allowed to + action?` (J.)

## 8. What we intentionally do NOT teach

To keep Scene 006 focused and reusable, it will NOT require memorising:

- a single universal security arrival deadline;
- whether passports or boarding passes are required at every security point (it varies by airport);
- the full liquids / electronics rules (Scene 007);
- prohibited-item tables or confiscation options (Scene 009);
- the mechanics of the secondary bag search (Scene 008);
- Fast Track pricing or availability as if it were standard.

The learner should instead leave Scene 006 able to:

1. recognise and follow the main security signs;
2. join the right queue / lane and politely confirm they are in the right place;
3. respond to short officer instructions (tray, pockets, step through, clear);
4. understand why an "additional check" might be asked and stay calm;
5. know the arrival-time advice exists and should be checked for their specific airport/flight.
